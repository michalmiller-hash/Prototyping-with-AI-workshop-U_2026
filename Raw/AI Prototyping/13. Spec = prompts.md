You often read that specifications are no longer written; instead, prototypes are built. 
A specification used to be a description of functionality—how something works, how it behaves, and the actions taken by both the user and the system. 
The shift that occurred was evolutionary rather than revolutionary. Nowadays, a feature's specification isn't created before the feature itself is built; it emerges during the process—through prompts that create and modify the prototype, and through the expanding context. 
So, a specification—like a prompt—is still a text-based description of how something works; it still answers the question "how." The difference is that, in the past, the specification largely remained confined to a document, whereas today it can materialize directly into software in real time, right before our eyes.
