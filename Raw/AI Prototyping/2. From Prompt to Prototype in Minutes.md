**Intro to Reforge Build**

Reforge Build is purpose-built for product teams who need to prototype and validate ideas quickly, turning concepts into working software that helps them brainstorm ideas, align teams, and validate concepts with customers.

![](https://cdn5.dcbstatic.com/files/r/e/reforge_docebosaas_com/1788184800/R30jyYFItmP8aqsz8_LeIQ/scorm/13c7a59b80838f515b3334984e5746b093c1baa4848e58c6b40e042f838020b2/scormcontent/assets/image.jpg)

When you open Reforge Build, you'll find two approaches to building:

- **Quick Mode** gets you to visual mockups faster, showing you different design directions right away. It's perfect when you have a clear idea of what you want and just need to see it realized.
    
- **Plan Mode** goes deeper in exploration, asking clarifying questions about your constraints and helping you think through tradeoffs before generating anything. This is better for sophisticated prototypes where the requirements are complex or you want the AI to help you work through edge cases.
    

For this first exercise, we'll use Quick Mode to go from idea to working prototype in a few minutes.

As a product manager, you'll create more prototypes than you expect—different variations, experiments, alternatives for stakeholder review and customer validation. A clean project structure from the start will save you headaches later.

Good organization is key, and Reforge Build makes it easy with Projects—essentially folders for all your related prototypes. This becomes especially important when you're managing prototypes across multiple product initiatives or sharing work with your team.

## Your first prototype — a Retrospective Board

Let's start with something practical that everyone PM: a team retrospective board. You've probably used tools like EasyRetro before. Now you can build your own, customized to your team's specific needs.

Here's the prompt we'll use:

_Build a team retrospective board with three columns:_

 _"What went well" (green)  
 "What didn't go well" (red)  
 "Action items" (blue)  
  
Users should be able to:  
1. Add new cards to any column  
2. Upvote cards they agree with  
3. See cards sorted by vote count within each column  
  
Use a clean, minimal design. Include 2-3 sample cards in each column to start_

This prompt demonstrates several best practices. It specifies the core functionality (three columns, adding cards, voting, sorting), provides design direction (clean and minimal), and includes sample data to make the prototype immediately usable.

![](https://cdn5.dcbstatic.com/files/r/e/reforge_docebosaas_com/1788184800/R30jyYFItmP8aqsz8_LeIQ/scorm/13c7a59b80838f515b3334984e5746b093c1baa4848e58c6b40e042f838020b2/scormcontent/assets/image%20%281%29.jpg)

It's remarkably terse—much shorter than a spec or user story. Yet it gives Reforge Build everything it needs to create a strong result. Later in this course, we'll explore how to craft effective prompts. Spoiler alert: prompt length has very little to do with quality.

## Using variations for brainstorming

Reforge Build has a unique feature: **Variations**. As a product builder, you’ll often want to explore multiple concepts before committing to a design. With **Variations**, Reforge Build generates four distinct concepts—select your favorite to generate a working prototype from that design direction. This transforms your initial prompt into a visual exploration, helping you quickly move from a rough concept to a specific approach.

In this case, we get four concepts:

- A traditional Kanban/Trello-style layout
    
- A stacked card system optimized for scrolling through lots of items
    
- A dark-themed variation with different visual treatment
    
- An expandable accordion view that collapses categories

## The Planning Phase: How AI Builds Complex Things

![](https://cdn5.dcbstatic.com/files/r/e/reforge_docebosaas_com/1788184800/R30jyYFItmP8aqsz8_LeIQ/scorm/13c7a59b80838f515b3334984e5746b093c1baa4848e58c6b40e042f838020b2/scormcontent/assets/image%20%2823%29.jpg)

Once you select a design, watch what happens. The tool creates a plan—a to-do list for the AI that breaks your project into discrete tasks:

- Create a column component with color theming
    
- Create a card component
    
- Add sorting logic
    
- Run validation build
    

This divide-and-conquer approach mirrors how we build software with our teams. It's not accidental. For LLMs to build complex things reliably, they must break projects into manageable pieces and implement them sequentially.

Understanding this process helps you work more effectively with these tools. When a prototype fails or produces unexpected results, it's often because a step in the plan went wrong. Understanding the plan helps you identify what broke and how to improve your prompt.

## What's Missing (and Why That's Fine)

This prototype has limitations. There's no user authentication—everyone's anonymous. Refresh the screen and everything resets.

These limitations are features, not bugs. Your first prototype isn't meant to be production software. It's meant to help you validate concepts, communicate ideas, and make decisions faster. Complex features at this stage may slow you down, making it harder to explore and iterate quickly.

We'll cover strategies for adding persistence and authentication in later lessons. For now, the ability to quickly test interaction patterns and gather feedback is valuable on its own—even without robust functionality.

## A Quick Tour of Reforge Build

Now, let me walk you through the key features of Reforge Build. Most prototyping tools will have an equivalent set of features.

## Version History

**Version History** is your safety net. Every change creates a new version. When an iteration doesn't work out—and some won't—you can roll back instantly. This freedom to experiment without fear of breaking things is essential. Use it liberally.

## Iterating On Your Prototype

### Prompt Attachments

Prompts don't have to be text only. You can attach screenshots, documents, or Figma designs to provide visual context. The **Attach files** feature lets you upload reference images, existing designs, or documentation showing how you want something to look or behave. You can also **Import from Figma** to transform design work into a functional prototype. The more context you provide, the closer the AI gets to your vision on the first try.

### Edit Mode

Not every change needs to go through the AI chat interface.

Edit Mode lets you select any element—text, buttons, containers—and modify properties directly. Change font sizes, colors, and spacing without writing a prompt. Because the interface is modular, changes to a component propagate everywhere that component is used.

This is particularly useful for design polish. Tweaking spacing, adjusting colors, and refining typography is faster through direct manipulation than through conversation. Save the chat interface for structural changes and new functionality.

![](https://cdn5.dcbstatic.com/files/r/e/reforge_docebosaas_com/1788184800/R30jyYFItmP8aqsz8_LeIQ/scorm/13c7a59b80838f515b3334984e5746b093c1baa4848e58c6b40e042f838020b2/scormcontent/assets/image%20%2815%29.jpg)

### Prompt Enhancement

**Prompt Enhancement** analyzes your prompts and suggests clarifications that help the LLM produce better results. It's a collaborator helping you ask for things more effectively—particularly useful when you're still learning what level of detail these tools need.

![](https://cdn5.dcbstatic.com/files/r/e/reforge_docebosaas_com/1788184800/R30jyYFItmP8aqsz8_LeIQ/scorm/13c7a59b80838f515b3334984e5746b093c1baa4848e58c6b40e042f838020b2/scormcontent/assets/image%20%2816%29.jpg)

## Code View

The **Code View** enables you to see the code underlying the working application. For our retrospective board:

- **App.tsx** contains the main application logic, initial data, and primary user interface elements
    
- **CSS files** provide all the styling in index.css
    
- **Component files** define reusable pieces like Card and Column, defined in the /components folder.
    

This view may feel intimidating if you've never worked with code before, but that's okay. Throughout this course, we'll walk through the code step-by-step so you can understand what drives your prototype. As you develop this understanding, you'll gain a better sense of why things work the way they do—which helps you create better prompts, iterate more effectively, and build more sophisticated prototypes.

The generated code is well-organized and modular. Each component—columns, cards, buttons—is defined separately. This matters because understanding the structure lets you make precise requests.

You don't need to be a developer to benefit from understanding what's being generated. Let me show you why.

For example, there's a Column.tsx file that defines the code for each of the three columns in our Kanban board: "What Went Well," "What Could Be Improved," and "Action Items."

Instead of saying "add a shadow to the columns," you can say "add a shadow to the Column component." In this simple case, the two are interchangeable. However, as your prototype grows more complex, plain English may create ambiguity when referring to specific components and concepts. By referencing the actual component name, you give the AI more precise instructions.

This precision makes iteration faster and more reliable. The AI is less likely to misunderstand your intent when you're specific about what you're changing.

## Your New Superpower

In a few minutes, we went from a short prompt to a working prototype. We explored design variations. We built functional software. We tested interactions.

This changes product development fundamentally. Until now, creating software has been expensive—and our entire product development process has been shaped by that constraint. Now, working software becomes part of how we communicate, make decisions, and validate ideas—not just what we ship at the end.

As we’ve seen, prototyping is easy. Maybe too easy. I've seen teams jump in enthusiastically, only to hit a wall when the initial excitement fades. They never answered the most important question: _Why are we prototyping this in the first place?_

Prototypes should be tools, not toys. Toys are cool demos that impress for 30 seconds. Tools are decision-making artifacts that help you ship better products.

Like any tool, prototyping works best when you understand not just how to use it, but when to use it and what you're trying to accomplish.

The rest of this course addresses two key questions:

1. How do you **build prototypes that are useful** to your product development process?
    
2. How do you know **when prototyping is the right tool**?
