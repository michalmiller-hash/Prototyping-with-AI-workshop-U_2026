# 



**Total time: ~1 hour**

**Objective:** Apply what you've learned by building a simple productivity tool prototype from scratch. This assignment will help you internalize the core concepts from this lesson and develop confidence working with AI prototyping tools.

## Part 1: Build Your Prototype (30-45 minutes)

**Choose one of these productivity tools to build:**

- **Daily Standup Tracker** — A simple form where team members can log what they did yesterday, what they're doing today, and any blockers
    
- **Reading List Manager** — Track articles or books you want to read, mark them as read, and add notes/ratings
    
- **Weekly Goal Planner** — Set 3-5 goals for the week, check them off as you complete them, and review at week's end
    
- **Meeting Notes Template** — A structured note-taking interface with sections for attendees, agenda items, decisions, and action items
    
- **Or your own idea** — Pick any simple personal productivity tool you'd actually use
    

**Requirements for your prototype:**

1. Use **Quick Mode** or **Plan Mode** in Reforge Build (or equivalent in another tool)
    
2. Explore at least **2-3 design variations** before committing to one
    
3. Include these elements:
    
    - At least one way to add/create new items
        
    - Some form of organization or categorization
        
    - Basic interactivity (buttons, clicks, state changes)
        
4. Test your prototype on at least **2 different screen sizes** (desktop + mobile or tablet)
    
5. Use **Edit Mode** to refine at least one visual element (color, spacing, typography)
    

## Part 2: Reflect & Document (15-20 minutes)

**Answer these questions in a short write-up:**

1. **Initial Prompt:** What was your initial prompt? How detailed was it?
    
2. **Iteration Journey:** How many iterations did it take to get something you were happy with? What changes did you make along the way?
    
3. **Surprises:** What did the AI do better than expected? What did it struggle with?
    
4. **Learning Moments:**
    
    - Did viewing the code help you understand anything about how your prototype works?
        
    - Did you use any specific component names when iterating? How did that help?
        
5. **Prototyping Value:** If you were building this for real with a team, what decisions would this prototype help you make? What questions would it answer?
    
6. **Limitations:** What's missing from your prototype that would be needed for a real product? (And why is it okay that these are missing?)
    

## Deliverables

- **Share link** to your working prototype
    
- **Short reflection** (250-500 words) answering the Part 2 questions
    

## Success Criteria

You've succeeded when:

- ✅ You have a **working, functional prototype** (not just a mockup)
    
- ✅ You've **tested the interactions** and verified they work as intended
    
- ✅ You can **articulate what decisions** this prototype would help a team make
    
- ✅ You understand the **limitations** of what you built and why they're acceptable at this stage
    

**Remember: Don't get too hung up on making everything work perfectly.** Your prototype will have rough edges. Features won't work exactly right. Critical functionality that would be non-negotiable in a production product will be missing—and that's completely fine. This assignment isn't about building production-ready software. It's about getting comfortable with the interaction model of AI prototyping tools and developing an intuition for how Reforge Build works. Focus on the process of prompting, iterating, and refining rather than achieving perfection.
